The menus

Three ways we set the table.

Every package is built around the thali: a set of courses that arrive together and get refilled until your guests say bas. Final menus are written with each family — these show how we structure them.

Gher nu jaman

For dawats, pujas & gatherings at home · 25–100 guests

  • One shaak of the season — ringna bateta, bhinda, or undhiyu in winter
  • Dal & bhat — Gujarati dal with steamed rice
  • Rotli — delivered soft, ghee on request
  • Farsan — khaman or patra, one kind
  • Athanu & chaas — pickle, papad, buttermilk

Prasang thali

For engagements, receptions & milestone birthdays · 100–350 guests

  • Two shaak — one dry, one gravy
  • Dal or kadhi — plus jeera rice or khichdi
  • Live rotli station — off the tava through the meal
  • Farsan counter — khaman, patra, samosa
  • One sweet — mohanthal, shrikhand, or basundi

How we cook

The kitchen rules we won't bend.

Gujarati food fails quietly: dal that's flat because the vaghar was rushed, rotli that stiffened in a hotbox, shrikhand that never got its saffron. Our rules exist to stop exactly those failures at volume.

We're a family operation finishing our commercial kitchen build-out now. Until it's licensed and inspected, we're writing menus with families for upcoming dates rather than taking deposits — the inquiry list below holds your date in line.

  • Cooked the morning ofNothing served at your prasang was made the day before. Shaak and dal finish on site or arrive within the hour.
  • Rotli made liveFrom the prasang thali up, rotli comes off the tava during the meal — never stacked an hour ahead.
  • Pure veg kitchen, no exceptionsOne kitchen, vegetarian only — no shared fryers or borrowed equipment from mixed kitchens. Jain variations cooked separately on request.
  • One wedding a dayWe don't split the kitchen across two halls. Your date gets the whole family.
